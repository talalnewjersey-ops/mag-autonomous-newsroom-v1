Best Way to Send Money to Mexico from the USA (2026): Wise vs Remitly vs XE

Quick Answer: There's no single "best" provider for every situation. Wise offers the most transparent exchange rate (true mid-market, no hidden markup) but only delivers by bank deposit — no cash pickup. Remitly has by far the widest cash-pickup network in Mexico (Elektra, OXXO, BanCoppel, Walmart, and dozens more) and the lowest disclosed flat fees. XE supports the largest single transfers ($535,000) and offers bank deposit, cash pickup, and mobile wallet. Your best choice depends on how your recipient will collect the money and how much you're sending.

The United States is the largest source of remittances to Mexico, and the corridor moves tens of billions of dollars a year — Mexico received $61.8 billion in remittances in 2025, according to Banco de México data reported by Mexico News Daily, with the U.S.-to-Mexico corridor alone sending about $2.5 billion less that year than in 2024, the first annual decline since 2013.

With that much money moving and dozens of providers competing for it, the fee and delivery-method differences between them are real money in your recipient's pocket — not just marketing copy. This guide compares Wise, Remitly, and XE specifically for the USA-to-Mexico corridor, using only information confirmed directly on each provider's own site.

How We Compared These USA-to-Mexico Transfers

We reviewed Wise, Remitly, and XE's official USA-to-Mexico corridor pages directly, rather than relying on third-party comparison sites, for payment methods, delivery methods, disclosed fees, disclosed exchange-rate methodology, transfer limits, and current promotions.

An important limitation, stated plainly: all three providers calculate final quotes (fee + exchange rate + amount received) through an interactive calculator that requires entering a real send amount and, in some cases, recipient details. We were not able to operate these interactive calculators to pull a full grid of quotes at $100, $500, $1,000, and $5,000 for every provider and every delivery method. Where a provider's own site displayed a specific, real example (most commonly at $1,000), we've used that exact figure and labeled it clearly. Where we could not obtain a verified figure, we say so rather than estimating — because a wrong fee number on a financial comparison is the kind of mistake that costs you real money. Which Service Gives Your Recipient the Most Pesos?

For a confirmed $1,000 transfer, the disclosed examples put Wise's cheapest option (direct debit) at a $7.67 fee on top of the true mid-market rate, while XE's bank-transfer example showed no additional fee at a transfer-specific rate slightly below the pure mid-market rate — meaning XE's cost is effectively built into its exchange rate rather than shown as a separate fee. Remitly's standard disclosed fees ($1.99–$3.99) are the lowest of the three, but its promotional exchange rate for new customers' first $1,000 is better than its own standard rate is likely to be — Remitly does not disclose its standard (non-promotional) markup, so we can't confirm how it compares to Wise or XE once the promotional period ends. The honest takeaway: Wise is the only one of the three that discloses its full pricing methodology (fee + true mid-market rate, nothing else), which makes it the easiest to verify and compare for yourself — even if it isn't necessarily the cheapest for every amount or method.

Wise: Best for Rate Transparency

Wise is the only provider of the three that explicitly commits to the real mid-market exchange rate with no built-in markup, charging its cost as a separate, disclosed fee instead. For a $1,000 transfer, Wise's own corridor page shows: direct debit $7.67, bank transfer $12.07, debit card $41.11, credit card $57.91, Apple Pay/Google Pay $66.99 — so payment method matters enormously to your total cost even though the exchange rate stays the same. The tradeoff: Wise delivers to Mexico by bank deposit only, via the CLABE account system, with no cash pickup or mobile wallet option, and it isn't available to Wise users based in Texas. Typical delivery is same-day to next-business-day depending on payment method.

Remitly: Best for Cash Pickup

Remitly's named partner network in Mexico is the most extensive of the three by a wide margin, including Elektra/Banco Azteca, BBVA Bancomer, BanCoppel, Santander, HSBC, Scotiabank, OXXO, Farmacias Guadalajara, Banorte, Walmart/Bodega Aurrera, and Mercado Pago, among others — each with its own dedicated confirmation page on Remitly's site. If your recipient doesn't have a Mexican bank account, or needs cash in hand quickly at a familiar local retailer, Remitly's network is the strongest match of the three. Standard disclosed fees are the lowest we found ($1.99 for bank/debit deposit, $3.99 for cash pickup), and new customers get a promotional exchange rate on their first $1,000 sent — though Remitly doesn't publish its standard, non-promotional rate markup, so confirm the live rate before your first transfer and again after the promotional period ends.

XE: Best for Large Transfers

XE supports online transfers up to $535,000 to Mexico — well above what we could confirm for Wise (roughly $95,000 via CLABE) or Remitly (roughly $100,000, per a Remitly blog post rather than a formal limits page). XE also covers all three delivery methods — bank deposit to major Mexican banks, cash pickup at 40,000+ locations (including Elektra, BanCoppel, BBVA, Soriana, Farmacias Guadalajara, and OXXO), and mobile wallet via the XE app — making it the broadest single-provider option if you want delivery flexibility without comparing multiple services. XE's live $1,000 bank-transfer example showed no separate fee, with the cost effectively embedded in a transfer-specific exchange rate slightly below the pure mid-market rate; XE does not publish its exact markup formula.

Which Is Fastest?

For cash pickup, both Remitly and XE advertise delivery in minutes once the transfer is processed — Wise doesn't offer cash pickup at all for this corridor. For bank deposit, Wise's direct-debit option is the fastest of the three we could confirm (74% of Wise transfers overall arrive in under 20 seconds, per Wise's own general speed claim; bank transfer specifically can take up to the next business day). XE's bank deposits are described as arriving "within hours" to major banks. Remitly offers Express and Economy speed tiers generally, though we could not confirm Express/Economy branding applies specifically to the Mexico corridor on Remitly's own site.

Hidden Costs to Watch

  • Card payments cost more everywhere. Wise's own numbers show a debit card ($41.11) or credit card ($57.91) costing 5–7x more than direct debit ($7.67) for the same $1,000 transfer — the exchange rate doesn't change, only the fee does.
  • A "no fee" transfer can still have a worse rate. XE's and Remitly's models often show $0 or low separate fees, but that doesn't mean the transfer is free — the cost can be built into a less favorable exchange rate than the pure mid-market rate. Wise is the only one of the three that separates these two costs completely and discloses both.
  • Promotional rates expire. Remitly's better-than-standard rate on your first $1,000 is a real, time-limited offer — not evidence of Remitly's ongoing rate quality. Re-check the live rate on your second transfer.
  • Delivery method changes your cost, not just your speed. Cash pickup convenience (Remitly, XE) can come with a higher flat fee than a bank deposit through the same provider.

How to Send Money from the USA to Mexico

  1. Choose a provider based on how your recipient will collect the money — bank account (any of the three), cash pickup (Remitly or XE), or mobile wallet (Remitly or XE).
  2. Create an account and verify your identity (typically a government-issued photo ID; U.S. providers are regulated Money Services Businesses registered with FinCEN under the Bank Secrecy Act).
  3. Enter the amount and your recipient's details — for a Mexican bank deposit, this generally means the recipient's 18-digit CLABE number; for cash pickup, the recipient's full name matching their photo ID.
  4. Review the exchange rate and fee shown for your specific amount and payment method before confirming — these numbers are quoted live and can differ from any example figures in this article.
  5. Pay via your chosen method (bank transfer, debit card, or credit card) and track the transfer using the provider's app or website.

What Information Does Your Recipient Need to Provide?

For a bank deposit, your recipient's full name (matching their ID) and their 18-digit CLABE account number are generally required. For cash pickup, your recipient typically needs a valid government-issued photo ID matching the name on the transfer, plus the transfer reference number you'll receive after sending. Requirements can vary slightly by provider and by amount — always confirm the exact requirements with your chosen provider before sending.

Is It Safe to Send Money to Mexico Online?

Wise, Remitly, and XE are all licensed Money Services Businesses registered with FinCEN in the United States, and are subject to federal consumer protections. Under the Electronic Fund Transfer Act (Regulation E), you generally have the right to cancel a remittance transfer for a full refund within 30 minutes of payment, per the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. As with any online financial service, verify you're on the provider's real website before entering payment details, and never send money to someone you haven't verified is the intended recipient.

Wise vs Remitly vs XE: Final Verdict

There's no single overall winner here — the right choice depends on your recipient's situation. If your recipient has a Mexican bank account and you want a fully transparent, verifiable exchange rate, Wise is the clearest choice. If your recipient needs cash in hand at a local retailer, or doesn't have a bank account, Remitly's partner network is the strongest match. If you're sending a large amount and want flexibility across bank, cash, and mobile wallet delivery from one provider, XE's higher limits and broader delivery options make it the most versatile. Whichever you choose, check the live quote for your exact amount and payment method before sending — the numbers in this guide are real examples, not guarantees.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the cheapest way to send money to Mexico from the USA?

Based on the fees each provider discloses, Remitly's standard fees ($1.99–$3.99) are the lowest we found, but Wise is the only provider that fully discloses its exchange-rate methodology (true mid-market, no markup) alongside its fee — so the true cheapest option depends on the exchange rate applied to your specific transfer, not just the advertised fee. Always compare the total amount your recipient will receive, not just the fee.

Can my recipient get cash in Mexico without a bank account?

Yes, through Remitly or XE, both of which offer cash pickup at retail and bank partner locations across Mexico (Elektra, OXXO, BanCoppel, and others). Wise does not currently offer cash pickup for this corridor — only bank deposit via CLABE.

How long does a transfer to Mexico take?

It depends on the provider, payment method, and delivery method. Cash pickup through Remitly or XE is typically available within minutes once processed. Wise's direct-debit bank deposits are often near-instant; bank-transfer-funded deposits can take up to the next business day. Always check the specific delivery estimate shown at checkout for your amount and method.

Is there a limit on how much I can send to Mexico?

Limits vary by provider: XE allows up to $535,000 online, Wise allows up to $95,000 via its CLABE bank-deposit option (with some Mexican banks excluded), and Remitly's own blog references a $100,000 single-transfer limit, though we could not confirm that figure on a formal terms page. For very large transfers, contact the provider directly to confirm current limits.

Do Wise, Remitly, and XE charge hidden fees?

Wise separates its fee from the exchange rate and discloses both, which is the most transparent structure of the three. Remitly and XE don't always publish their standard exchange-rate markup, so a "$0 fee" or low-fee transfer can still cost more overall if the exchange rate is less favorable than the pure mid-market rate — compare the total amount your recipient receives, not just the advertised fee.

Conclusion

Wise, Remitly, and XE each do one thing better than the other two: Wise on transparency, Remitly on cash-pickup reach, and XE on transfer size and delivery flexibility. None of them is the objectively cheapest for every amount and method — the honest answer is that your recipient's situation (bank account or not, how fast you need it there, how much you're sending) should drive your choice more than any single "best overall" ranking. Check the live quote for your specific transfer before sending, since rates and promotions change.
